LINKS 2026

Language Models, Information, Networks, Knowledge, and Society

Working across disciplines to understand how language models are reshaping the information environment, public discourse, and society.

About

Large language models are increasingly embedded in search engines, social media feeds, news platforms, chat interfaces, and emerging agentic systems, where they mediate how people navigate health topics, social issues, and political debates. They may democratize access to information and lower barriers to civic participation, but they can also embed ideological and cultural biases, generate and amplify misinformation, exhibit sycophantic behavior, distort information-seeking, and reshape online information ecosystems.

Research on these effects remains fragmented: NLP and ML researchers often focus on system development, benchmarking, safety, and evaluation, while political scientists, communication scholars, sociologists, information scientists, and computational social scientists often study downstream effects in real-world settings. This workshop uses the information environment as an integrating frame to bridge these communities and foster the interdisciplinary community needed to study LLM-mediated information systems rigorously and govern them responsibly.

Themes & Topics

The workshop invites contributions on, but not limited to, the following themes:

  • Information-seeking, epistemic autonomy, and AI companions Search and chatbot audits, over-reliance, sycophancy, health behavior, learning, and fact-checking.
  • Political bias and civic discourse Bias measurement, persuasion, deliberation, participation, and election information.
  • Social media, misinformation, AI agents, and moderation Synthetic actors, AI influencers, information integrity, and amplification dynamics.
  • Audits, evaluation, and governance Benchmarks, red-teaming, policy approaches, disclosure, accountability, and public-interest oversight.

Submissions

We welcome single-blind, non-archival extended abstracts of up to four pages (excluding references and appendices) via OpenReview. Accepted submissions will be presented as posters, spotlights, or contributed talks. The workshop has no proceedings: submissions should present ongoing, preliminary, or otherwise non-archival work, and work already published or accepted at NeurIPS or other ML venues should not be submitted. Important dates will be announced soon.

We are committed to supporting junior researchers and will actively recruit speakers, panelists, and program committee members across genders, geographies, seniority levels, disciplines, and institution types.
